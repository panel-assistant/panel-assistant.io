## Capable hardware, let down by its software

Android wall panels are genuinely good hardware wrapped in genuinely bad software. The dashboard lags, taps vanish, the vendor's own app elbows its way onto the screen, and every make demands a different app and a different ritual to set it up. Most people simply endure it, because a panel with no buttons and no dependable reset is an expensive thing to break.

Panel Assistant is the free and open-source cure. It turns any Android wall panel into an appliance that runs your existing Home Assistant dashboards quickly and keeps running them, with one consistent way to set up and manage every panel you own, and nothing to nurse, tune or tweak by hand.

## Getting a panel up, whatever shape its firmware is in

Most panels arrive from an overseas marketplace with a thin manual and no promise that anything beyond the vendor's own app will ever run on them. You discover whether it works only after the money and the evenings are spent. Panel Assistant is meant to take that uncertainty away: [check your model](/install/supported-panels/) before you buy, and know that once it arrives there is a tested, repeatable way to get it running.

The hard part of any panel is the first hour. Panel Assistant does that hour for you, from inside Home Assistant. If the panel is already on your network, give the integration its address and it does the rest. If it is still in the box, plug it into your laptop with a USB cable and install straight from your browser, before it ever goes on the wall. Either way you approve one prompt on the panel and watch it through.

## Not on the list? Describe it, and it is

Hardware support in Panel Assistant is data, not code. Every model is described by a profile: how to recognise the panel, what hardware it carries, and which routes are safe for driving its screen, LEDs, relays and sensors. Profiles are plain text, edited and validated in your browser on the panel itself, with no development tools and no rebuild of the app. A panel the project has never seen still gets the essentials from the conservative Generic profile, and from there you, or the manufacturer, can teach it the rest and share the result with everyone.

Profiles describe what a panel might have; the running panel proves what it actually has. Anything that cannot be verified is shown with a lock and an explanation rather than a broken control, a profile that fails on startup rolls back automatically to the last one that worked, and the screen always finds the safest route it can, from a true backlight-off down to a gentle dim. Nothing is ever faked, and nothing that works is ever left on the table.

## Performance you can measure, not just feel

Cheap panels are slow for reasons, and Panel Assistant shows you the reasons. The panel's own status page measures the real latency of your taps, time to interactive, main-thread blocking, unexpected reloads, state updates per second and the three entities responsible for most of them, alongside CPU, GPU, memory, clock speed, temperature and the busiest processes. A likely-cause row reads all of that and tells you what is holding the panel back and how sure it is. When you want to compare a change, an anonymised snapshot leaves out every identifier so it can be shared safely.

## Fit it, forget it

A wall panel should behave like a light switch: always there, never needing you. Panel Assistant learns the normal light in the room over the week and dims and brightens with it, records a manual adjustment as a temporary preference rather than fighting you, and learns its proximity sensor's own baseline so a wave wakes the screen and a passing cat does not. Overnight it freezes the dashboard while the screen is off, saving most of the panel's processing, and quietly releases accumulated memory before morning. A dashboard that keeps crashing is contained rather than restarted all night, a Home Assistant outage is shown plainly on the panel and cleared only when Home Assistant is genuinely back, and the last good dashboard reopens by itself after a restart.

## Vendor software, tamed

The software that came with your panel is not going to help, and Panel Assistant is built for that. It takes over the home screen and gives a button-less panel an on-screen way to get around. It knows that most panels ship with a browser engine years too old to draw a modern Home Assistant dashboard, keeps a curated collection of known-good versions, and on supported panels can install one for you. When a vendor's control widget insists on drawing itself on top of your dashboard, Panel Assistant can keep it out of the way, while refusing by design to touch anything the panel needs to stay alive. And because every panel's hardware was worked out by hand rather than taken from the vendor's closed libraries, the whole product can be given away as unrestricted open source, with no account, no cloud and no per-device licence.

## New, and moving fast

Panel Assistant is new and evolving quickly. Three panel families have full hardware support today and several more are documented, and the goal is universal support for every Android wall panel worth mounting. That takes real hardware in real houses, which is where you come in.

- **Star the project on GitHub.** It is the simplest way to tell other Home Assistant users this exists. [The integration](https://github.com/panel-assistant/ha-integration) is where it starts.
- **Report your panel.** If your model is not fully supported yet, say so, with the model name and what worked, in the [issues](https://github.com/panel-assistant/ha-integration/issues). Better still, share the profile you taught it.
- **Tell people.** A mention in the Home Assistant community, on a forum thread or in a video, reaches the people still nursing a laggy panel.

## Free, open, and yours

Everything here is free and open-source software under permissive licences, with no account, no cloud service and no subscription. It is built by a Home Assistant user who wanted wall panels to work the way the hardware always promised, and it is shared so that yours can too.
