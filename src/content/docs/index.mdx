<Split title="Capable hardware, let down by its software">

Android wall panels are genuinely good hardware wrapped in genuinely bad software. The dashboard lags, taps vanish, the vendor's own app elbows its way onto the screen, and every make demands a different app and a different ritual to set it up. Most people simply endure it, because a panel with no buttons and no dependable reset is an expensive thing to break.

Panel Assistant is the free and open-source cure. It does one job: run your Home Assistant dashboards on the wall, quickly and without fuss. Every panel you own is set up and managed the same way, from Home Assistant, with nothing to nurse, tune or tweak by hand.

</Split>

<Spotlight
  title="Only the entities your dashboard uses"
  figure="10–100×"
  caption="less Home Assistant state traffic reaching the panel, measured in real homes"
>

A dashboard on a wall panel normally receives every state change in the house, whether it shows that entity or not. On a large installation a low-powered panel spends its time decoding thousands of entities it never displays, and that is usually why it lags.

Panel Assistant's entity filter learns which entities your dashboard really depends on, by reading its configuration and by watching the dashboard while it runs, then asks Home Assistant to send only those. Home Assistant does the filtering before anything is sent, so the rest never reaches the panel.

It is the difference between a panel that lags and one that answers the moment you touch it. [See how it works and how to turn it on](/manage/performance/).

</Spotlight>

<Split title="Getting a panel up, whatever shape its firmware is in">

Most panels arrive from an overseas marketplace with a thin manual and no promise that anything beyond the vendor's own app will ever run on them. You discover whether it works only after the money and the evenings are spent. Panel Assistant is meant to take that uncertainty away: [check your model](/install/supported-panels/) before you buy, and know that once it arrives there is a tested, repeatable way to get it running.

The hard part of any panel is the first hour. Panel Assistant does that hour for you, from inside Home Assistant. If the panel is already on your network, give the integration its address and it does the rest. If it is still in the box, plug it into your laptop with a USB cable and install straight from your browser, before it ever goes on the wall. Either way you approve one prompt on the panel and watch it through.

</Split>

<Split title="Panel not on the list? Describe it, and it is">

Hardware support in Panel Assistant is data, not code. Every model is described by a profile: how to recognise the panel, what hardware it carries, and which routes are safe for driving its screen, LEDs, relays and sensors. Profiles are plain text, edited and validated in your browser, with no development tools and no rebuild. A panel the project has never seen still gets the essentials from the conservative Generic profile, and you can teach it the rest and share the result.

</Split>

<Split title="Performance you can measure">

Cheap panels are slow for reasons, and Panel Assistant shows you the reasons. The panel's status page measures tap latency, time to interactive, main-thread blocking, unexpected reloads, state updates per second and the three entities responsible for most of them, alongside CPU, memory and temperature. A likely-cause row reads all of that and tells you what is holding the panel back.

</Split>

<Split title="Fit it, forget it">

A wall panel should behave like a light switch: always there, never needing you. Panel Assistant learns the normal light in the room and dims with it, learns its proximity sensor so a wave wakes the screen and a passing cat does not, releases memory overnight while the screen is off, and reopens the last good dashboard by itself after a restart.

</Split>

<Split title="Vendor software, tamed">

Panel Assistant takes over the home screen and gives a button-less panel an on-screen way to get around. It keeps known-good browser engines for panels that ship one years too old, and keeps a vendor's control widget from drawing over your dashboard, while refusing by design to touch anything the panel needs to stay alive.

</Split>
