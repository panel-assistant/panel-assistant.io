Panel Assistant 1.0.3 is for panels on a separate network, such as a VLAN or an IoT network, where firewall rules sit between the panel and Home Assistant. Discovery now offers a panel at whichever of its addresses answers, a panel already running can be added at any address, an install that cannot reach the panel on port 8888 says so after about a minute, and when a panel loses its connection to Home Assistant the log says so and you get a way back to network ADB. These notes cover what you will notice since 1.0.2; if something does not work on your panel, please open an issue with the panel model and what you saw. A few of the newest messages are in English for now.

Pairs with the 1.0.3 Android app, which each panel is offered as an update. Panels on the 1.0.2 app keep working.

### New

- **One discovered card for a panel heard through network ADB.** A panel heard through Android's network ADB announcement at one address and through the Android app at another could show two cards under Discovered. With the 1.0.3 Android app, the panel reports the serial number of its network ADB, and the network ADB card closes, or never appears, once a panel you added or a waiting discovered card reports the same serial number. Panels on older apps behave as before.
- **WebView updates for a panel without internet.** With the 1.0.3 Android app, Panel Assistant downloads a panel's WebView update from panel-assistant.io itself and hands it to the panel, so a panel that cannot reach the internet can still update its WebView, as long as Home Assistant can reach panel-assistant.io. The file is checked against the expected size and checksum, downloaded once for all panels that need it, kept out of Home Assistant backups and deleted an hour after its last use; if the panel cannot use it, the panel downloads the update itself as before. When a panel cannot download its WebView update, Panel Assistant now raises the WebView Repair, which says to let Home Assistant or the panel reach panel-assistant.io, and writes what stopped the download to the log, instead of treating the update as not published yet.

### Changed

- **A way back to network ADB.** When a panel lost its connection to Home Assistant, Panel Assistant fell back to the panel's network ADB and waited for it to open, which never happened while the Android app was running, and then asked you to turn ADB on. It now reads the panel's status first and stops at once: removing a panel, repairing its permissions after an update and the Repair for allowing updates each say that the Android app keeps network ADB off and how to get it back. That is to let the panel reach Home Assistant again, to restart the panel, or, with the 1.0.3 Android app, to turn on Network ADB under System on the panel's own settings page once the panel has been without Home Assistant for five minutes; the message says how many minutes are left. The cause is written to the Home Assistant log.
- **Your permission choices on the panel are kept.** Installing or updating the Android app granted the camera, microphone and notification permissions, and allowed Modify system settings, Display over other apps and Install unknown apps, even when someone had turned them off on the panel. Panel Assistant now grants only what nobody has decided and leaves a choice made on the panel as it is. When an update leaves something turned off, one Repair for that panel names the features that will not work, links to the Android permission guide and clears itself at a later update that finds nothing turned off; if you meant to turn them off, select Ignore. When it installs the app over USB, Panel Assistant now keeps any permission the panel's owner has turned off instead of switching it back on, and the install no longer fails because of it.
- **Panel setup changes are made once per install or update.** After a panel's own setup finished, Panel Assistant made its app a device administrator and applied its settings for that make of panel from its regular poll, and did both again after every Home Assistant restart and whenever the panel's address changed, which undid a choice made on the panel, such as turning a Meta Portal's package verifier back on. These changes are now made only when Panel Assistant installs or updates its app, so a setting you change back on the panel stays as you left it until the next update. The install steps now say that on a Meta Portal, Panel Assistant turns off Android's package verifier so that its app can update itself, which means the Portal stops checking that new apps were signed by Meta before installing them, and that removing Panel Assistant from the panel turns the verifier back on. The README lists what setup changes on each make of panel.

### Fixed

- **Discovery finds a panel at the address that answers.** A panel that announced itself at both an IPv6 and an IPv4 address, on a network that drops IPv6 between Home Assistant and the panel, was never offered if its IPv6 announcement arrived first. Discovery now tries each address the panel announces, IPv4 first, and offers one card at the address that answers. A new panel that Home Assistant hears but cannot reach at any address is now written to the log as a warning naming every address and port tried and the error, instead of being dropped without a word.
- **Add a running panel at any address.** Adding a panel that was already running at an address outside the local network ranges, such as a Tailscale address or a public IPv6 address, was refused as if it were an install. Panel Assistant now adds a running panel at any address, without opening a network ADB connection to it. If no panel answers on port 8888 there, the form says so and explains that network installs go only to a private local IPv4 address or a unique-local IPv6 address.
- **An install says when port 8888 is blocked.** When a firewall let network ADB through but blocked port 8888, the install waited between three and twelve minutes with nothing in the log, and then asked for a verification nobody could do. It now ends after about a minute and says that Panel Assistant is installed on the panel but Home Assistant could not reach it on port 8888, for example because of a firewall or VLAN rule, and that once that is allowed you add the panel again with the same address, with no need to reinstall. The Repair says the same.
- **A panel that cannot reach Home Assistant says so.** A panel that Home Assistant could reach, but that could not reach Home Assistant, showed Not signed in to Home Assistant and a Repair asking you to sign in, which could not help. Panel Assistant now asks the panel whether it holds a sign-in, and when it does, shows Cannot reach Home Assistant and a Repair naming the address the panel is trying and the host and TCP port to allow, with links to the panel's Logs page and its connection setting, and writes one warning to the log. With the 1.0.3 Android app, the panel's own screen also shows the address it is trying.
- **A lost connection is logged.** When a panel's connection to Home Assistant ended, nothing was written to the log, and a panel that had connected once never got a Repair for a lasting loss. Home Assistant now writes a warning naming the panel and the reason when the connection ends and does not come back within a minute, and a line when it returns. A panel that keeps answering but stays without a connection now raises a Repair after two minutes, as a panel that never connected does.
- **A panel that calls in but cannot be reached is named.** A panel that connected to Home Assistant while a firewall stopped Home Assistant from reaching it was refused without a word, and adding it at its own address said nothing answered there. Home Assistant now writes a warning naming the panel and the addresses it reports, once until they change, and when nothing answers on port 8888 at one of those addresses, adding the panel there says that it has connected but Home Assistant cannot reach it on port 8888, and to allow TCP port 8888 in your firewall or router. A panel you removed that keeps calling is noted in the log without a warning.
- **Adding a panel names the port that did not answer.** Adding a running panel whose port 8888 refused the connection, or never answered, said the panel answered and asked you to turn on Android Debug Bridge. The form now says what happened on port 8888: the connection was refused, no usable answer came back, or something other than the Android app answered. It explains that port 8888 is where the Android app answers Home Assistant and port 5555 is used only to install it, and the log says the same.
- **Bare IPv6 addresses are accepted.** Adding a panel by an IPv6 address typed without square brackets now works.
- **Install names ADB when it stops answering.** If the panel's network ADB closed, or stopped allowing this Home Assistant, between the install preview and pressing Install, the install said the panel identity or clean package state had changed. The panel had not changed, so it now says that nothing answered on the Android Debug Bridge port, or that the panel has not accepted this Home Assistant, as the step before it does.
- **An unfinished install is found by the panel, not only its address.** An install left waiting at the panel's setup step kept only the address it used. Adding the same panel at its other address, IPv4 instead of IPv6 or the other way round, or from a discovered card heard there, missed the unfinished install and added the panel, and finishing the install later made a second entry for one panel, with a false identity Repair. Panel Assistant now asks the panel at each waiting install's address for its id and offers the unfinished install when it matches, and a finished install whose panel already has an entry becomes that entry. Unfinished installs are now picked up when Home Assistant starts, even with no panel added yet, so a first install cut off by a restart ends by itself.
- **Setup is read where the install reached the panel.** A panel added by a DNS name other than its own host name was installed at the address that name resolved to, but its finished setup was then read by the name, which the panel refuses. Every Submit showed the same setup form, with no error and nothing in the log. The setup is now read at the address the install used, and a panel that refuses the read is shown on the setup form with what it answered, and logged.
- **A poll finishes when the connection ends midway.** When a panel's connection to Home Assistant ended in the middle of a poll, the poll failed, logged an error about the panel's health and marked the panel unavailable, while the panel still answered on the network. The poll now finishes over HTTP, as it does for a panel without a connection.
- **An install failure Repair clears when the panel is added.** After an install ended asking for a verification, adding the panel as a found panel connected it but left the install failure in Repairs. With the 1.0.3 Android app, adding that panel now clears the Repair. The panel is matched by its network ADB serial number alone, never by address, so another panel at the same address clears nothing; with an older Android app, select Clear error as before.
- **The address Repair no longer says the panel has moved.** When a firewall let a panel reach Home Assistant but not the other way, the Repair said the panel had moved and its new address could not be confirmed, and its first step was to add the panel again, which fails behind that firewall. It now says that Home Assistant cannot reach the panel at its stored address or at the address its connection reports. The first step is to let Home Assistant reach the panel again; adding it again comes second, only if it has moved.
- **An update that leaves the app stopped recovers in about a minute.** When a panel's Android app stopped after an update installed and did not start again, Panel Assistant waited up to nine minutes before starting it over network ADB. It now starts the app once the panel has been silent for a minute.
- **Large transfers no longer pause Home Assistant.** Checking an app download, a stored panel backup, a shared wake-word model or an upload from your browser, and writing the update's temporary file, happened all at once on Home Assistant's event loop, so a file of hundreds of megabytes could pause every integration on a small host. Uploads are now checked piece by piece as they arrive, and the rest runs in the background.
